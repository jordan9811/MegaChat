# MegaChat brief — 2026-07-27 (evening run)

## Objectives
- Ship last night's work to production and prove the storage question for good
- Build the missing front door: let a fan actually record and fund a MegaChat
  for a streamer who isn't here yet
- Answer the "did our Twitch app survive the domain move" question with
  evidence, not guesses

## The headline
**A fan can now do the thing the bounty program promises.** Pick a streamer,
record a MegaChat in the browser, watch it back, re-do it if it's bad, set the
money and an expiry, see exactly what the rejection rules are, and pay — in
that order, payment last, so nobody ever pays for a take they threw away. The
whole journey was driven end to end in a real browser by an automated test:
record through a camera, pay, check the status page, claim as the streamer,
review the clip, approve it, and watch the fan's status flip to Approved.

## Also built
- **One bounty, several doors.** A fan can offer the same bounty to up to three
  streamers — first to claim takes all of it. The dangerous part isn't the
  race (that's handled: two simultaneous claims were fired at it and exactly
  one winner came out, every time) — it's the *display*. A streamer's page now
  always leads with what's exclusively theirs, and contested money is labelled
  with how many rivals are racing for it. Nobody claims a pool and finds it
  half gone without having been told.
- **Expiry.** Fans set how long their offer stands (default about a week).
  Nothing is ever locked forever: lapse means an automatic full refund, and
  the recording is deleted along with it.
- **The streamer is the editor.** Nothing airs without their approval. Their
  queue sorts the safe stuff first (the same AI review the room clips use now
  grades bounty clips at upload — never during a live broadcast), and
  rejecting asks *why*: "not for me" refunds the fan fully with no penalty;
  "breaks the rules" counts against repeat offenders — second offence refunds
  half, and the withheld half goes into the streamer's own pool, never to us.
- **Claiming is real now (when switched on).** With one setting, claiming a
  handle requires being signed in with the Twitch account that owns it —
  proven by Twitch, not typed into a form. Testing this immediately caught a
  nasty bug: a *failed* claim used to jam the handle so nobody — including
  the real owner — could claim it afterwards. Fixed.
- **Audience size is now captured at the moment it exists.** Every time a clip
  plays on a claimed Twitch channel, we record how many people were watching.
  Payouts should scale with audience, and that number is impossible to look up
  after the broadcast ends.

## The domain question, answered honestly
- The address our code sends Twitch is derived from the live site, so the code
  side literally cannot be stale: it sends `https://megachat.fun/auth/twitch/callback`.
- The app's credentials work (proven with a real token grant), and the stream
  preview embed cannot break from the domain move (proven against a live
  channel with 55,000 viewers, plus a negative control).
- What I could NOT prove from outside: whether the Twitch developer console
  has the new address registered. Twitch only checks it *after* login — my
  first probe nearly reported success on evidence that turned out to prove
  nothing, and a deliberate wrong-address test exposed that. **Your
  60-second test: while logged into Twitch, visit megachat.fun/auth/twitch.**
  If it bounces you back to megachat, the console is fine; if Twitch shows a
  mismatch error, add the address (it's written out in the audit doc).
- Also learned: everyday Twitch *login* goes through Privy's own app now, so
  even a stale console entry wouldn't have broken sign-in. Kick has no keys
  yet; nothing was guessed.

## Problems, stated plainly
- **Creating a fresh account costs nothing, so the rejection penalty is soft.**
  Strikes attach to whatever account string a fan types. Someone determined to
  probe the content filter can just use a new name. The fix is requiring
  sign-in to pledge (and eventually keying penalties to the payment method) —
  flagged for a decision rather than quietly half-built.
- A pre-existing test for the room-clip moderation pipeline turns out to have
  been broken on trunk before tonight (proven by re-running without my
  changes). Filed; the new moderation path has its own working coverage.
- Last night's volume question is now closed for good: the boot marker written
  by one deploy was still there when the next deploy started. Storage
  survives, proven, not inferred.
