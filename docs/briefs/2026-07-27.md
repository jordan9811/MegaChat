# MegaChat brief — 2026-07-27

## Objectives overnight
- Ship yesterday's approved work to production and confirm it is actually serving
- Fix the two holes where things quietly disappear: the spend meter forgetting on
  every deploy, and whatever was happening to fan recordings
- Make alarms reach a person instead of a log file
- Then find whatever else matters, unsupervised

## The headline
**The bounty feature — fans record clips for a streamer who isn't on MegaChat
yet, streamer claims and plays them, everyone gets paid — had no clips in it.**

Not "clips that don't last". None. The contribution record had a field pointing
at a recording, and nothing anywhere ever read that field. No part of the bounty
flow ever accepted a video file. And a MegaChat is recorded *into a room*, which
a streamer who hasn't signed up doesn't have — so there wasn't even a place to
put one.

The escrow, the payout maths, the anti-cheating watermark, the verification —
all of that was real and working. It was all keeping careful accounts of
something that did not exist. A streamer who did everything right would have
claimed their handle, installed the overlay, gone live, and had nothing to play.

Built the missing piece: recordings are now stored durably, held for the 90 days
the product promises, and the streamer can actually fetch and play them back.
Tested the whole way through — a fan contributes, uploads a recording, the
streamer claims, and the exact bytes come back out the other end.

**Second one, found by walking the same path end to end:** the moment a streamer
tried to go live, the server threw an error. A function had been deleted in an
earlier redesign and one caller was left behind pointing at it. Every test
missed it because they all set up sessions by reaching past the web layer
instead of going through it. Fixed, plus a check that now catches that whole
category — if anyone deletes a function and leaves a caller, a test fails
immediately instead of a streamer finding out.

## Also fixed
- **The spending cap could be bypassed by deploying.** The meter kept everything
  in memory, so every deploy reset the day's total to zero. A leak that spanned a
  restart was invisible to the exact system built to catch leaks. It now
  remembers across restarts, and on startup it *asks the video service* who is
  actually connected rather than guessing. When it can't ask, it deliberately
  errs toward over-counting — an early false alarm is recoverable, a missed leak
  is not.
- **We were never actually checking whether storage survives deploys.** The
  health check just confirmed a setting was filled in, which proves nothing. It
  now leaves a marker and looks for it on the next boot, so the answer is
  evidence instead of an assumption.
- **Alarms now reach you.** Budget warnings, the long-session alarm, and storage
  filling up can post to Discord or Slack. It stays quiet when nothing is set up,
  never breaks anything if the destination is down, and won't spam you if
  something flaps. Fired a real test message and watched it arrive.
- **Three follow-ups from the OBS black-screen fix**, one of which was quietly
  rebuilding the original expensive bug: in a locked-down OBS the overlay was
  getting a new identity on every reload, and since it reloads itself whenever
  the connection drops, that meant stacking up billed connections. Also: two
  overlays open now cost double, so that's detected and shown to you rather than
  hidden.
- **Confirmed your Twitch thumbnail actually shows up**, not just that the
  setting saves. Your room is carrying the Twitch channel in production right
  now; the picture appears when you're live.

## Problems and how we handled them
- I got the test-quality audit wrong on the first pass and corrected it — I
  flagged 48 cases as weak because they didn't import our code directly. They
  drive the real thing over the network against real money, which is the
  strongest kind of test, not the weakest.
- One of my own new checks reported a bug that wasn't there: it was reading a
  comment I'd written *about* the deleted function and treating it as real code.
- Nearly committed a file that would have made the storage check permanently lie
  — it would have reported "storage is safe" on a fresh machine with no storage
  attached. Caught and excluded.

## Outstanding
- **Nothing lets a fan actually record a bounty clip yet.** The storage and the
  plumbing are in; the record-and-send screen isn't built. I stopped rather than
  invent a new product screen unsupervised. **This is now the single thing
  standing between the bounty feature and being usable.**
- **You: paste a Discord or Slack webhook address into the settings** so alarms
  reach your phone. Two minutes, and then hit the test button to confirm.
- **You: confirm the hosting service isn't set to sleep when idle.** Carried over.
- **Still no way to reverse a payout once it's sent.** I wrote up the options and
  recommended one — hold back about 20% of each payout for two weeks, so
  reversing is just "don't send the rest" rather than chasing someone. Not built;
  it's your call.
- The payment path is still tested against a copy of itself rather than the real
  thing. It's now checked for drift, but properly fixing it needs a funded test
  wallet.

## Outlook: bullish, with one caveat sharpened
- Two genuinely serious defects found and fixed, both of which would have been
  discovered by the first real streamer instead of by us. That is the good
  version of a bad night.
- The money-safety work is now real rather than assumed: the cap survives
  deploys, storage survives deploys *provably*, and alarms leave the building.
- The caveat is sharper than yesterday, not softer: the bounty mechanic is
  closer to working than it has ever been **and** we now know it has never once

  "all the parts are built" and "the thing works" are different claims.
