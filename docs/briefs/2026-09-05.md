# 2026-09-05 — Guest list

## Objectives
- Let a streamer name people who can join their stream free, any time.
- Prove they are genuinely never charged, and that nobody else can copy the trick.

## What happened
Streamers can now keep a guest list. Add someone by their MegaChat name and
they walk into any of your rooms without paying, without waiting for a seat,
and without ever seeing a payment screen. It is one list per streamer, not per
room, so a co-host is added once and works everywhere you go live. There is a
master switch to turn the whole thing off for a while without losing anyone,
and a limit of 20 guests so it cannot quietly become a free room.

Two decisions worth knowing about. A guest gets an **extra** seat rather than
one of the paid ones, so they never wait and never push out someone who paid.
And taking someone off the list never interrupts them mid-stream: their
current session finishes and the change applies the next time they join.

It is proven rather than believed. A test suite drives the real website the
way a person would, on a real server, and checks 53 separate things — including
watching every single request the server makes to the payment network and
confirming that not one of them moves money. That check was itself wrong at
first and passed for a bad reason; it was fixed and now measures nine real
requests and confirms none of them are payments.

**This is on a branch and is not live.** Nothing on megachat.fun changed today.

## Problems and how we handled them
- **The money check was lying.** The first version of the "nobody was charged"
  test watched the wrong address, so it saw zero payment traffic simply because
  it was looking at nothing. Found and fixed before it could be trusted; it now
  proves the point instead of assuming it.
- **A separate test appeared to pass and shouldn't have.** While checking for
  damage to existing features, one of the older test scripts passed on the
  current code and failed on the new work. It turned out neither result was
  real: that script waits 2.5 seconds for the site to start, and on this machine
  the site takes over two minutes. Its earlier "pass" came from an abandoned
  copy of the site left running from a previous session. Roughly seventeen older
  test scripts share this flaw. Not caused by today's work, now written down,
  and the fix is mechanical.
- **Most of the older tests therefore could not be run here** to confirm nothing
  broke. Three that use the newer, reliable startup could, and all three passed.

## Outstanding
**Owner**
- Decide whether the guest list ships. Nothing is deployed. (Say the word.)
- Still open from before: set `AUTH_SECRET` in Railway. Until then the live site
  signs logins with a value that is published in the public code, which means
  someone could impersonate an account. **Two minutes**, and it is the highest
  priority item on this list.

**Engineering**
- Port the seventeen fragile test scripts onto the reliable startup helper.
- If a streamer ever uses a large guest list on a busy stream, the extra seats
  are a real video-hosting cost; worth a smaller cap on how many guests can be
  live at once.

## Outlook
**Mid, leaning bullish.** The feature is small, self-contained, and better
evidenced than most of what is in the repo — the payment-network check is
stronger proof than a log line. What holds it at mid is that the older test
suite could not be run here, so "nothing else broke" rests on three tests plus
reading the code, not on the full set. Porting those scripts would move this to
bullish quickly, and the login-secret item outranks all of it.
